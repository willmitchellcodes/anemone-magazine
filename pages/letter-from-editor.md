“Are you having any fun?”

I began to ask myself this question in a prospective advertiser’s ski gear shop, a few months into launching *Anemone*. 

Of course, this whole thing—putting together a literary magazine by and for Montanans—has been an extraordinary undertaking. Still, advertiser rejections began to gnaw at me, however ironic. Not because I wonder whether independent publishing is worth the effort, but because perception can easily be mistaken for purpose.

As far as I can surmise, “reading” and “not a good fit” in the same breath reflects a broader hesitation toward unvarnished, authentic work. That’s enough railing against my deficiencies as a salesman packaged as a treatise on culture.

Shut up, man. What of the writing?

This project began with a call for entries. Scores of writers from around the state answered with remarkable vulnerability. Telling trends emerged. For one, the plurality of work concerned some facet of outer space, wilderness, memory, and community.

To winnow that pile to just thirteen finalists, our editorial team sought equal parts craft, conviction, and resonance.

Perhaps that’s all we can do: laugh when the laughter is at our expense, write honestly, and keep printing. Thank you for reading.
